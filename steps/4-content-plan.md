# Step 4. Content plan

Every part of the session gets a destination before any cut, written in `Work/plan.md`.

**A piece belongs to whoever gives the value.** If one guest asks and the other answers, the answer goes to the one who answered. Every person gets pieces alone, not only shared ones. When guests interview each other, each conversation feeds both their accounts.

## Horizontal (16:9)

| Format | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Episode | the whole conversation, tight | whole thoughts, no repeats, ums and dead air out |
| Spicy cut | roughly half | a cold open of the best hooks, then the tightest version |
| LinkedIn social clip | 30 to 60 s | hook, one clear point, spoken CTA, professional tone |

## Vertical (9:16)

| Format | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Social clip, per person | 30 s to 1:10 (aim 45 to 60) | hook, middle, spoken CTA; only that person's words |
| Social clip with the host | 30 s to 1:10 | the guest gives the value, the host asks or reacts |
| Team social clip | 30 s to 1:10 | several people; the named person opens and carries it |
| Mini interview | 30 s to 1:10 | question, answer, invite to follow |
| Story | 15 to 30 s | one moment |

**Team layouts (vertical):** 2 people, one on top and one on the bottom. 3 people, one on the bottom and two side by side on top. The person talking moves to the bigger spot; reactions and two people talking share the two boxes (Step 6).

## Getting as many social clips as possible

1. Go through the transcript person by person and list every moment where they give something: a story, an opinion, a tip, a confession, a laugh, a strong one-liner.
2. Each moment with a hook, a middle and an ending becomes a clip, closed on that person's real spoken CTA from anywhere in the session.
3. One strong moment can feed several formats: a vertical clip, a LinkedIn clip, a story.
4. A clip over 1:10 gets cut down, never shipped long.

## Story rules for every short piece

- The hook is the first sentence and stops the scroll on its own. No context openers ("she's my daughter, we were talking about...").
- The middle keeps the point and loses filler and repeats.
- The CTA is a real ask that person said in that session. Never invented. If none exists, end on their strongest line and say so in the plan.
- A setup always gets its payoff: "I have a proposal" is followed by the proposal.
