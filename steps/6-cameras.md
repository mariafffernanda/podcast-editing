# Step 6. Cameras and framing

**Who is on screen**

1. The person talking is on screen from their first word. When unsure who's talking, use the wide: you never go wrong with it.
2. Reactions are wanted, but only inside one person's turn: at least 1 s after it starts, 1 s before it ends, 1.5 to 2.5 s long. A reaction never covers someone else starting to talk.
3. Both talking, laughing together, a shared moment: the wide (horizontal) or the split layout (vertical).
4. Never a listener on their phone, looking down at notes, or someone crossing the lens.

**Cuts**

5. Every jump cut changes camera, even a tiny breath cut. Never a jump on the same frame.
6. A simple cut between phrases on the same camera keeps the exact same framing: one crop per camera per person per video, never re-centred shot by shot.
7. No zoom spam. Never hide a jump cut with a punch-in: change camera.
8. Simple cuts only. No dissolves, no fades.
9. No shot under about 1 s, no hold over about 14 s in conversation.

**Framing**

10. Don't crop in more than about 1.7x on 1080p footage; tighter looks soft.
11. Normal headroom: eyes on the upper third.
12. Take the widest crop that stops before the neighbour's body, and check a still before rendering.
13. A vertical piece of one person uses that person's own camera, never a crop of the wide.
14. No empty chairs, no stray arms, phones or water bottles at the edge.

## One shot or two shot

| Moment | Shot |
|---|---|
| One person talks, the others listen quietly | one shot of the talker |
| A real reaction (laugh, nod, surprise, emotion) while someone talks | two shot of talker and reactor, or a short reaction cut inside the turn |
| Two people talk at once or go back and forth fast | two shot |
| Everyone laughs, a shared moment | wide (horizontal) or the boxes (vertical) |
| Unsure who is talking | wide or two shot, never a guess |
| An emotional key line (a tear, a confession) | one shot, held, no cutaway |

Reactions are found from the sound (a laugh or "wow" on the listener's own mic) and the face (a smile, a nod), and listed in the plan with their times so a human can add or drop them. In vertical team clips the talker takes the bigger box, and the two boxes share the screen for reactions and crosstalk.

## Zooms

- Only on a line that matters: a key point, a punchline, an emotional moment.
- Rare: about one a minute at most, never two in a row, never the same person twice running.
- Slow and subtle, held for the whole sentence; never used to hide a jump cut.
- The plan lists every zoom with the line it serves. A zoom with no reason comes out.
